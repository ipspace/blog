Finding good content in the swamp of AI slop is hard, and whenever I find a blog post worth reading, I'd like to know when the author publishes the next one.

For stupid reasons (like a day having only 24 hours, and me having a life), I can't periodically scan all websites I find interesting, but fortunately, I don't have to. Two well-known mechanisms work reliably: email updates and RSS feeds.
<!--more-->
{{<note info>}}
Ideas like "I'll publish links on my social media" make us slaves to the engagement-farming algorithms, and there's no guarantee those algorithms would show me stuff I would want to see anyway.

While it doesn't hurt to publish links to your content on social media, I got so little traffic from Twitter that I [stopped publishing stuff](/2023/05/goodbye-twitter/) the moment they started charging for the API.

LinkedIn is a bit better (it's the top referring site, responsible for ~6% of the incoming sessions), but at least I get some insightful comments (plus the mandatory dose of AI slop) because it's easier to write them there than on my blog.
{{</note>}}

Imagine my dismay when I found an [interesting blog post](https://vstaub.com/blog/the-future-of-network-engineering-is-bigger-than-networking/) published on the [owner's own website](https://vstaub.com/) (nice), powered by a pretty minimalistic HTML generator[^PA] (awesome), but without an RSS feed.

{{<note update>}}
2026-09-04 14:30 UTC: The RSS feed on the website that triggered this blog post appeared within a few hours (it's already added to my RSS reader). Thanks a million, great job!
{{</note>}}

[^PA]: Probably [this one](https://docs.astro.build/en/basics/layouts/)

Even worse, there's no documented way to contact the [author](https://vstaub.com/about/) to tell him to add it to his site, and I refuse to go through the hassle of "_Add a LinkedIn contact just so you can send him a short message_".

If you invest time into creating great content (thanks a million!!!) and decide not to feed the trolls[^MF] (awesome decision BTW), pretty please do us old-timers a favor and add an RSS feed to your site. Most static website generators already have a template; you just have to enable/add it to your site.

[^MF]: Medium, LinkedIn, Facebook, and friends
